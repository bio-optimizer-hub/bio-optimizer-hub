Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

What is enterocyte permeation and why does lymphatic bypass matter?

Enterocyte permeation refers to how molecules pass through the absorptive cells lining the small intestine. Standard fat-soluble molecules enter the portal vein and undergo rapid hepatic first-pass metabolism by CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 enzymes, destroying most of the active compound. Lymphatic bypass packages the molecules into sub-45nm micelles that are absorbed directly into the lymphatic system, protecting them from hepatic degradation and significantly increasing plasma bioavailability.

How does sub-45nm micellar technology achieve 36% AUC bioavailability?

By engineering carrier micelles to be smaller than 45 nanometers, the formulation drastically increases the surface area-to-volume ratio. This allows rapid paracellular and transcellular transport across the enterocyte membrane, yielding an area under the curve (AUC) bioavailability of up to 36%, compared to single-digit percentages found in standard oils.
