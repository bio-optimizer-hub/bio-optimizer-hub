Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

How do hepatic CYP2C19 polymorphisms affect cannabinoid clearance?

CYP2C19 is a key Phase I hepatic enzyme responsible for the oxidation of various compounds. Genetic variants such as *2 and *3 create poor metabolizer phenotypes, leading to slower clearance and prolonged systemic exposure, whereas *17 alleles drive ultra-rapid metabolism, reducing the duration of action.

Why is first-pass metabolism a barrier in standard cannabinoid delivery?

Standard oral ingestion routes active compounds through the portal vein directly to the liver, where enzymes like CYP2C9, CYP3A4, and CYP2C19 metabolize and degrade a large percentage of the active molecules before they ever reach systemic circulation.

How does sub-45nm lymphatic micellar absorption work?

By encapsulating cannabinoids into micellar structures smaller than 45 nanometers, the molecules are absorbed directly by intestinal enterocytes into the lymphatic system. This clever delivery mechanism completely bypasses the portal vein and initial hepatic degradation, elevating systemic AUC by up to 36%.

What is the pharmacological relevance of nationwide legal 0.00% THC formulations?

Formulations entirely devoid of THC eliminate psychotropic liability while utilizing minor cannabinoids and flavonoids—such as CBG for alpha-2 adrenergic mPFC agonism, THCV as a neutral CB1 antagonist, and Apigenin for GABA-A and CD38 modulation—to precisely target neurological and metabolic pathways.
