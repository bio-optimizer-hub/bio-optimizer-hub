Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

Why does caffeine induce jitters at the neurochemical level, and how does CBG counteract this differently than L-Theanine?

Caffeine antagonizes presynaptic A1 and A2A adenosine receptors, removing the natural 'braking' system on central catecholamines. This triggers an uncontrolled surge of norepinephrine from the locus coeruleus, precipitating somatic tachycardia, peripheral vasoconstriction, and subjective anxiety. L-Theanine mitigates this primarily by blocking glutamate binding at NMDA receptors, buffering excitotoxicity. In contrast, Cannabigerol (CBG) targets the upstream driver: it functions as a potent alpha-2A adrenergic receptor agonist in the medial prefrontal cortex. By activating presynaptic alpha-2A autoreceptors, CBG directly instructs noradrenergic neurons to decrease release of norepinephrine, arresting hyper-sympathetic jitters at the source while sharpening signal-to-noise ratio in executive circuits.

What is the biological significance of sub-45nm micellar lymphatic delivery versus traditional oral oil tinctures?

Conventional, lipid-dissolved cannabinoids swallowed in an oil vehicle must pass from the gut into the portal vein, where they are subjected to aggressive phase-I oxidation by hepatic cytochrome P450 enzymes—predominantly CYP2C9 and CYP3A4. This hepatic first-pass metabolism degrades up to 85–90% of the active compound, yielding unpredictable bioavailability between 6% and 10%. By encapsulating CBG and companion phytocompounds in a sub-45nm micellar architecture, the molecules bypass portal vein filtration entirely. Instead, they are transported via intestinal lacteals directly into the mesenteric lymphatic system, emptying into the systemic bloodstream via the thoracic duct and achieving an exceptional 36% systemic area-under-the-curve (AUC).

How do THCV, Apigenin, and Citicoline complete the multi-target neurochemical stack?

Neuro-Flow uses a poly-pharmacological approach: While CBG suppresses adrenergic jitters, THCV acts as a neutral antagonist at CB1 receptors, preventing metabolic sluggishness and promoting fronto-striatal dopamine signaling for clean, clean-burning executive motivation. Apigenin acts as a non-sedating positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors while concurrently inhibiting the CD38 ecto-enzyme, thereby preventing the depletion of neuronal NAD+ during sustained cognitive tasks. Finally, Citicoline (CDP-Choline) supplies cytidine for cellular membrane maintenance and free choline to synthesize acetylcholine, the primary neurotransmitter responsible for attention, working memory, and sensory gating.
