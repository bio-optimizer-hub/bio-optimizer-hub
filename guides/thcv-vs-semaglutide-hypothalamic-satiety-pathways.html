Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

How does THCV differ from Semaglutide in its mechanism of action?

Semaglutide is a synthetic peptide that mimics glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) to stimulate insulin secretion and suppress appetite via brainstem and hypothalamic receptors. THCV, conversely, operates as a neutral antagonist at the cannabinoid CB1 receptor within the endocannabinoid system, dampening the orexigenic signaling driven by anandamide and 2-AG.

What is the significance of sub-45nm micellar absorption for cannabinoids?

Standard oral cannabinoids suffer from extensive first-pass hepatic metabolism via CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 enzymes, severely limiting bioavailability. Sub-45nm micellar delivery encapsulates the active molecules into water-soluble structures that absorb directly via the lymphatic system, bypassing the liver and elevating AUC (Area Under the Curve) bioavailability significantly.
