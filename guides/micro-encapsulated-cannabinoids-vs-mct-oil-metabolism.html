Why does MCT oil result in poor cannabinoid bioavailability?

MCT oil acts as a standard lipid carrier, routing absorbed compounds directly through the portal vein to the liver. There, hepatic enzymes belonging to the cytochrome P450 family—specifically CYP2C9 and CYP3A4—extensively metabolize the active compounds into inactive metabolites before they ever reach systemic circulation, resulting in sub-6% real-world systemic exposure.

How do micro-encapsulated cannabinoids bypass the liver?

By engineering particle sizes to sub-45nm dimensions using advanced micellar delivery systems, the molecules mimic natural dietary fat digestion products. Instead of entering the portal vein, they are packaged into chylomicrons within the enterocytes and absorbed directly into the intestinal lymphatic system, bypassing first-pass hepatic clearance entirely.
