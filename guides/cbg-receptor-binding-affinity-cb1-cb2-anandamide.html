Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

How does CBG binding affinity at CB1 and CB2 compare to endogenous anandamide?

While anandamide is a potent endogenous orthosteric agonist for both CB1 and CB2 receptors, CBG behaves largely as a modulator, exhibiting low-affinity antagonism at CB1 and partial agonism at CB2. This prevents the psychoactive over-activation commonly associated with full agonists while fine-tuning tone across the endocannabinoid system.

What is the significance of alpha-2 adrenergic agonism in the medial prefrontal cortex?

Beyond classic cannabinoid receptors, CBG interacts with alpha-2 adrenergic receptors located in the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC). This pathway supports executive function, focus, and anxiolysis without sedative side effects, operating synergistically with endocannabinoid signaling.

How do Apigenin, THCV, and CBG interact pharmacologically?

THCV acts as a neutral CB1 antagonist, mitigating excessive appetite stimulation. Apigenin provides synergistic GABA-A receptor potentiation and CD38 enzyme modulation, promoting profound cellular relaxation and amplifying the neurobiological efficacy of the matrix.

Why is sub-45nm lymphatic micellar absorption superior to standard ingestion?

Standard oral cannabinoids suffer from extensive degradation via hepatic first-pass enzymes (specifically CYP2C9 and CYP3A4), yielding low bioavailability. Sub-45nm micellar delivery packages the compounds into structures small enough to enter the intestinal lymphatic system directly, avoiding liver degradation and boosting systemic AUC to 36%.
