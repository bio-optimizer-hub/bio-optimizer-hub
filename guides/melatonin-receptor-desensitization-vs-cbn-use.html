Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

What is the biological mechanism underlying melatonin receptor desensitization?

Exogenous melatonin acts as a high-potency full agonist on the G-protein coupled receptors MT1 (which decreases neuronal firing in the suprachiasmatic nucleus) and MT2 (which shifts phase timing). When introduced in supraphysiological doses (frequently 1mg to 10mg), the receptors undergo continuous hyper-stimulation. This triggers G-protein receptor kinase (GRK) phosphorylation and the subsequent recruitment of beta-arrestin proteins. Beta-arrestin sterically blocks further G-protein interaction and directs the MT1/MT2 receptors into clathrin-coated pits for internalisation (endocytosis). Consequently, receptor density on the membrane decreases dramatically, causing progressive clinical tolerance, reduced natural sleep drive, and paradoxical insomnia when unsupplemented.

Why does continuous CBN use avoid the rapid tolerance profile seen with melatonin?

Cannabinol (CBN) is a mild, non-intoxicating oxidation product of THC that exhibits a low-affinity partial agonism at both CB1 and CB2 cannabinoid receptors. Because CBN does not evoke maximal G-protein conformational changes or hyper-recruit beta-arrestin proteins to the same degree as full CB1 agonists or high-affinity synthetic ligands, the rate of receptor internalisation remains low. Continuous night-to-night administration supports somnogenic networks and peripheral relaxation without inducing the tachyphylactic tolerance or downregulating native receptor density typical of the MT1/MT2 axis.

How do CBG and THCV support the neuro-pharmacology of a CBN-dominant sleep architecture?

Targeted sleep signaling requires more than simple central sedation. Cannabigerol (CBG) contributes critical alpha-2 adrenergic mPFC agonism, acting on inhibitory presynaptic autoreceptors in the medial prefrontal cortex to throttle down nocturnal norepinephrine output, thereby lowering hyperarousal and physiological anxiety. Concurrently, THCV acts as a neutral CB1 antagonist at physiological micro-doses, balancing CB1 over-stimulation, stabilizing nocturnal glycemic signaling, and dampening reward-seeking wakefulness, yielding a balanced, non-disturbed entry into slow-wave non-REM stages.

What role does Apigenin play across GABAergic and CD38 pathways?

Apigenin acts synergistically via two distinct molecular pathways. First, it exerts positive allosteric modulation on central GABA-A receptors at a site distinct from classical benzodiazepines, promoting chloride influx, neuronal hyperpolarization, and anxiolysis without dependency. Second, Apigenin functions as a potent inhibitor of the CD38 ecto-enzyme, which is the primary consumer of cellular NAD+. By inhibiting CD38 during the nocturnal cycle, it prevents age-associated nocturnal NAD+ attrition, supporting sirtuin activity, cellular repair, and optimal mitochondrial recovery during deep sleep.
