Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

Why does conventional unformulated CBN oil consistently fail to alter Oura Ring deep sleep metrics?

Conventional CBN in lipid carriers (like standard MCT or hemp seed oil) suffers from low water solubility and severe first-pass hepatic metabolism by cytochrome P450 enzymes (specifically CYP2C9 and CYP3A4). This limits absolute bioavailability to roughly 4-6% and causes an erratic pharmacokinetic curve with a delayed Tmax of 2 to 4 hours. Because the deepest slow-wave sleep (N3) occurs predominantly during the first two sleep cycles (the first 3 to 4 hours of the night), conventional oils fail to deliver peak plasma concentrations when the brain's delta-generating machinery requires them most.

How do sub-45nm micellar nano-emulsions achieve a 36% AUC bioavailability profile?

When an emulsion is engineered below 45 nanometers, it bypasses the conventional lipid digestion cascade that relies heavily on gross emulsification and portal vein uptake. Instead, these sub-45nm micelles directly penetrate the intestinal enterocyte border and are preferentially packaged into chylomicrons. This shunts the active cannabinoids directly into the mesenteric lymphatic system, emptying directly into the subclavian vein. By completely dodging hepatic first-pass metabolism, systemic bioavailability reaches ~36% AUC with a sharp, predictable Tmax under 30 minutes.

What neurochemical roles do CBG, THCV, and Apigenin play alongside CBN in sleep architecture?

Sleep is a multi-system orchestration, not a single-receptor event. While CBN acts as a low-affinity CB1 partial agonist to facilitate sleepiness, Cannabigerol (CBG) acts on alpha-2 adrenergic receptors in the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), turning down sympathetic outflow and lowering pre-sleep heart rate. Micro-dosed THCV acts as a neutral CB1 antagonist, preventing cannabinoid receptor desensitization and mitigating residual morning sedation. Apigenin binds to positive allosteric sites on GABA-A receptors to quiet hyperactive corticolimbic circuits while simultaneously inhibiting CD38 enzymes to preserve the cellular NAD+ pool necessary for nighttime microglial and neuronal repair.

Does CBN use result in down-regulation of endogenous endocannabinoid tone or REM sleep rebound?

No. Unlike high-affinity full CB1 agonists like delta-9-THC, which heavily suppress rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, blunt slow-wave sleep depth over time, and cause severe REM rebound upon cessation, CBN displays a fractional intrinsic activity at CB1. Because it acts primarily as a weak partial agonist and is administered in precise nano-doses alongside neutral CB1 antagonists, it avoids receptor internalization and downregulation, maintaining endogenous anandamide and 2-AG signaling homeostasis without disrupting natural REM sleep architecture.
