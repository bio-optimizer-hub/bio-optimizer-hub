Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

How does THCV differ from THC regarding psychoactivity during a fast?

THCV (Tetrahydrocannabivarin) acts as a neutral antagonist at the CB1 receptor, contrasting sharply with THC which is a potent CB1 agonist. Because it does not activate the receptor, THCV is entirely non-psychoactive and does not induce the hyperphagia (the "munchies") typically associated with cannabis, making it ideal for maintaining an intermittent fasting protocol.

Why is Dihydroberberine superior to standard berberine extract?

Standard berberine suffers from notoriously poor intestinal absorption and rapid first-pass elimination. Dihydroberberine is chemically reduced, allowing for vastly superior gastrointestinal absorption and intracellular accumulation, resulting in equivalent metabolic efficacy at a fraction of the standard dose.

What is the significance of sub-45nm lymphatic micellar delivery?

Standard oral lipophilic compounds are degraded by hepatic enzymes via first-pass metabolism (specifically CYP2C9 and CYP3A4), destroying a large percentage of active molecules. Utilizing a sub-45nm lymphatic micellar delivery system encapsulates the active compounds into nano-sized micelles that are absorbed directly into the lymphatic system, achieving up to a 36% increase in area under the curve (AUC) systemic exposure.
