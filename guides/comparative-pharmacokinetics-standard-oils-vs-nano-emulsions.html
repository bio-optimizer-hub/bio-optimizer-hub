Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

What accounts for the low systemic bioavailability of traditional oil formulations?

Cannabinoids and lipophilic flavonoids administered in non-emulsified lipid carriers rely entirely on endogenous bile salt solubilization in the duodenum. The resulting large oil droplets undergo slow hydrolytic cleavage, followed by absorption through enterocytes directly into portal venous circulation. This direct pathway routes the payload into the liver, where phase I monooxygenases (predominantly CYP2C9 and CYP3A4) extensively hydroxylate and metabolize the compounds before they reach systemic vascular circulation, leaving systemic bioavailability between 4% and 8%.

How do sub-45nm micellar emulsions achieve 36% AUC bioavailability?

Droplets formulated below 45nm in hydrodynamic diameter possess an exceptionally high surface-area-to-volume ratio, facilitating immediate paracellular and transcellular permeation across the enteric epithelial layer. Instead of entering the portal venous microvasculature, these structured nano-carriers mimic endogenous chylomicrons and selectively partition into the lacteals of the mesenteric lymphatic system. By traveling through the cisterna chyli and entering venous circulation directly via the thoracic duct, the payload circumvents initial hepatic clearance, yielding an absolute systemic AUC of approximately 36%.

What is the biological mechanism of CBG on the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC)?

Unlike traditional cannabinoids that act predominantly as orthosteric agonists at cannabinoid type 1 (CB1) and type 2 (CB2) receptors, cannabigerol (CBG) displays dual affinity as a competitive alpha-2 adrenergic receptor agonist and a 5-HT1A receptor antagonist. Within the medial prefrontal cortex, postsynaptic alpha-2A adrenergic receptor activation stabilizes hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated (HCN) channels, reducing basal noise and optimizing signal-to-noise ratio in pyramidal networks that govern attention, working memory, and sustained vigilance.

How does neutral CB1 antagonism by THCV differ from an inverse agonist?

A neutral CB1 antagonist binds to the orthosteric binding pocket of the cannabinoid receptor and sterically blocks endogenous ligands (such as anandamide and 2-AG) from initiating signaling, without shifting the receptor into an inactive conformational state. Inverse agonists, by contrast, down-regulate constitutive, basal G-protein coupling below resting levels, which has historically produced depressive phenotypes and dysphoria. THCV maintains basal receptor integrity while modulating energy metabolism, satiety cascades, and insulin sensitivity.

What dual pharmacodynamic action does Apigenin provide across neuronal and energetic pathways?

Apigenin functions via two synchronized mechanisms. Centrally, it binds to a specific allosteric site on the GABA-A receptor complex distinct from conventional benzodiazepine loci, potentiating hyperpolarizing inward chloride influx to induce anxiolysis without cognitive sedation. Peripherally and centrally, it serves as a potent small-molecule inhibitor of CD38, an ecto-enzyme responsible for intracellular NAD+ degradation, thereby upregulating sirtuin-mediated metabolic defenses and mitochondrial energy efficiency.
