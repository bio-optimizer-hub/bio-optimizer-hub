Peer-Reviewed Science & Protocol FAQs

How does CBG influence the heart rate variability (HRV) metrics tracked by a Whoop strap?

Wearables like the Whoop strap calculate autonomic tone primarily using rMSSD (root mean square of successive differences between adjacent heartbeats) during non-REM slow-wave sleep. CBG functions as an agonist at central alpha-2A adrenergic receptors localized on presynaptic noradrenergic terminals within the locus coeruleus and medial prefrontal cortex. By stimulating these inhibitory autoreceptors, CBG suppresses excessive central norepinephrine discharge. This downregulates the sympathetic efferent drive to the sinoatrial node, allowing acetylcholine-mediated parasympathetic tone via the vagus nerve to dominate, manifesting objectively on a Whoop strap as increased nocturnal rMSSD and an elevated recovery percentage.

What is the biological necessity for sub-45nm micellar delivery over conventional MCT oil formulations?

Raw cannabinoids are highly lipophilic molecules (logP > 6) that present negligible aqueous solubility within the luminal fluid of the small intestine. Standard oil emulsions rely entirely on endogenous bile salts to produce coarse micelles (>500nm), leading to erratic absorption and extensive phase I oxidative metabolism in the liver by cytochrome P450 enzymes (specifically CYP2C9 and CYP3A4). This first-pass clearance can degrade over 85% of the ingested molecule before it reaches systemic circulation. Engineering CBG into stable sub-45nm lipidic micelles facilitates immediate passive diffusion through the mucosal unstirred water layer into intestinal enterocytes, packaging the active molecules into chylomicrons that enter the lymphatic lacteals directly. This route completely bypasses portal hepatic circulation, reliably yielding a 36% systemic AUC profile.

Why is THCV included alongside CBG for autonomic balance instead of relying solely on anxiolytic compounds?

Traditional anxiolytics often induce a blunt reduction in central nervous system activity, causing subjective sluggishness and depressing daytime energetic output. THCV serves a unique pharmacological role as a neutral antagonist at the orthosteric CB1 site. At calibrated low doses, it prevents the down-regulatory, sedating feedback loops typical of CB1 agonists, maintaining mental acuity, daytime focus, and healthy glucose homeostasis. When coupled with the prefrontal adrenergic calming effects of CBG, THCV acts as a neurological counterbalance, ensuring the autonomic nervous system retains functional flexibility (high vagal tone without loss of alertness).

What is the targeted mechanism of Apigenin in relation to parasympathetic tone and cellular energetics?

Apigenin possesses a dual-target mechanism: it functions as a positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors, potentiating the inhibitory effect of endogenous gamma-aminobutyric acid without inducing desensitization, while simultaneously acting as an inhibitor of CD38. CD38 is a dominant membrane-bound enzyme that consumes nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+). By inhibiting CD38, Apigenin preserves intracellular NAD+ pools, supporting mitochondrial ATP synthesis and sirtuin-mediated metabolic repair during rest phases. This molecular preservation matches perfectly with the restorative phase signaled by Whoop deep-sleep architecture.
